# Keyword research: Austin and nearby suburbs

Researched on 2026-11-23, as the follow-up to `seo-research.md`.

## How it was done, and its limits

- **What people type:** Google's public autocomplete, queried for 219 seed searches: card processing, merchant services, POS, card readers, dual pricing, surcharging, brands and devices. Each was paired with Austin, Austin TX, near me, Texas, Round Rock, Cedar Park, Georgetown and San Marcos, plus question and comparison phrasings. Autocomplete shows that people search a phrase, not how often.
- **Who ranks:** Bing's top organic results for ten main searches. Google answered with a CAPTCHA ("unusual traffic") and was not used. Bing is not Google, but it shows which kinds of sites own a search.
- **Not available:** search volumes, keyword difficulty and Google's local pack. Volumes need Google Keyword Planner (free with a Google Ads account) or a paid tool such as Semrush or Ahrefs. Treat every priority below as a judgment, to be checked against volumes and then against Search Console once the site has a few weeks of data.

## What people search

**City-level demand is thin; "near me" carries local intent.** "credit card processing austin tx" is a real phrase. Austin and suburb versions of most other terms return no payment suggestions at all; "card reader austin" mostly returns tarot readers. Searchers type "near me" (credit card processing near me, pos systems near me, clover pos systems near me, credit card machine near me) and let Google supply the location. That points to a strong Business Profile and few, well-chosen suburb pages.

**Business-type searches are the strongest buying signal.**
- POS: pos system for small business, restaurants, retail, food truck, bars, liquor store and nail salon.
- Processing: best credit card processing for small business, nonprofits, restaurants, medical office, dental office and contractors.
- Hardware: credit card machine for small business; mobile card reader for small business.

**Cost questions:**
- how much is a pos system for a small business, for a restaurant, per month

- how much are credit card processing fees

**Pricing models:**
- what is interchange plus pricing

- interchange plus vs flat rate

**Texas compliance:**
- is dual pricing legal in texas
- is credit card surcharging legal in texas

**Switching and comparing:**
- clover vs square (and for small business, and fees)

**Brands and devices:**
- clover pos pricing, fees, reviews

These match the hardware catalog's device pages.

**New business owners:**

- mobile food vendor permit austin
- austin public health food truck permit
- sales tax permit texas: how much, how long, do you need one
- business license austin tx

**Noise to avoid:** "merchant account" also brings up business banking (business bank account near me), which is out of scope for this site. "card reader" brings up tarot and SD cards. "surcharge" brings up ATM fees.

## The biggest gap: page titles

The browser-tab titles carry no search terms. Examples: "Pricing | Impeccabyte", "Restaurants & Bars | Impeccabyte", "In Person | Impeccabyte", "Clover Flex | Impeccabyte". The H1s are creative headlines, such as "Faster tables, cleaner tips, smaller fees." Keep the headlines, and give each key page its own search title. ## New content worth writing (in order)

1. **Texas surcharging and dual pricing rules.** A sourced section or FAQ on the Dual Pricing page. It answers "is surcharging legal in texas", "are debit card surcharges legal in texas" and "dual pricing vs cash discount". Small competitors and a state library page rank today. Keep the existing "not legal advice" line and cite Texas sources.
2. **Processing fees, in plain English.** Pricing FAQs for "what is the going rate for credit card processing", "why are credit card processing fees so high" and "how to reduce credit card processing fees", leading to the free statement review.
3. **Switching from Square.** A page for merchants outgrowing a flat rate: when it pays to switch, what moves over, and how dual pricing or interchange plus compares. It catches "square alternatives" and "switching from square to clover" with a local angle.
4. **Device price and spec answers.** The device pages already show prices and specs. With better titles, they can catch "clover flex price" and "pax a920 pro price" searches from local buyers.
5. **Opening a business in Austin.** A short checklist that links to the official city and state pages: sales tax permit, food truck and mobile food vendor permits, and business license. It ends at "your first merchant account". Keep it tied to payments, because AI Overviews will answer the general how-to.
6. **Suburb pages, only with local substance.** Autocomplete shows almost no suburb-level demand. Start with Round Rock, and Cedar Park or Georgetown, only once there are real local merchants to talk about. Skip the rest.

## Local listings that rank on their own

BBB, Yelp, YellowPages, chamberofcommerce.com and the Round Rock Chamber all appear in the top results for local payment searches. Listings there do double duty: they rank themselves and point to the site. Use the same name, website and service area everywhere. No street address can be shown, since the Houston mailbox is mail only.
